# Visual Instruction Tuning — the LLaVA Recipe
> Large Language and Vision Assistant (LLaVA) showed that you do not need a new architecture to
> get a capable vision-language model (VLM) — you need **a frozen vision encoder, one linear
> projection into the language model's token space, and instruction data**. Stage one aligns the
> projector on captions; stage two fine-tunes on visual instructions generated by prompting a
> text-only language model with captions and boxes. Two stages, a few hundred GPU-hours, and the
> open VLM era started.

**Why it matters:** the projector-plus-language-model recipe is still the default way an open
VLM is built in 2026, and the interview question "how would you add vision to an LLM?" wants
exactly this answer.

- **The single idea:** image patches become *tokens the language model already knows how to
  read*; the projector is the translator, and it is the only thing that must be trained first.
- **What interviewers probe:** why stage one freezes almost everything, what the instruction
  data actually contains, and why a multilayer perceptron (MLP) projector beat a linear one in
  LLaVA-1.5.
- **The failure modes people get wrong:** instruction data generated by a text-only model
  inherits its hallucinations, so the VLM learns to describe plausible objects that are not in
  the image; and a low-resolution encoder silently caps every text-in-image task.

**Start here — suggested path:**

1. **Get the two-stage picture** — read [Understanding Multimodal LLMs](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-multimodal-llms) — **Sebastian Raschka**. *The clearest side-by-side of the decoder-only (projection) and cross-attention approaches, with diagrams.*
2. **Read the paper that started it** — read [Visual Instruction Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485) — **Liu et al. (2023)**. *How the instruction data was synthesized, and how little architecture there is.*
3. **See what fixed it** — read [Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA-1.5)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744) — **Liu et al. (2024)**. *The MLP projector, academic-task data, and prompt formatting; a masterclass in cheap, honest ablation.*
4. **Hear it from the authors** — watch [Large Multimodal Models (CVPR 2024 tutorial)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S0CpenMvG48) — **Chunyuan Li**. *A LLaVA co-author on what the recipe does and does not buy you.*
5. **Fine-tune one yourself** — follow the [visual question answering task guide](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/en/tasks/visual_question_answering) — **Hugging Face**. *A working loop on a small VLM makes the data format concrete.*

## Key Papers

- [Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.08485) — **Liu et al. (2023)** — the recipe: CLIP encoder, linear projector, language-model-generated instruction data, two training stages.
- [Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning (LLaVA-1.5)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03744) — **Liu et al. (2024)** — MLP projector, higher resolution and academic data; the version most later work forked.
- [LLaVA-OneVision: Easy Visual Task Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03326) — **Li et al. (2024)** — single-image, multi-image and video in one model; where the recipe scaled to.
- [BLIP-2: Bootstrapping Language-Image Pre-training with Frozen Image Encoders and Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12597) — **Li et al. (2023)** — the Q-Former alternative to a plain projector; the comparison LLaVA is usually judged against.
- [A Survey on Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13549) — **Yin et al. (2024)** — the taxonomy of connectors, training stages and data that this page sits inside.
